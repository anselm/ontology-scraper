# 2026-10-14 — Framing: who this is for, what the screens are, milestones

Point-in-time entry, written before any code, from Anselm's brief of
2026-10-14 and the predecessor projects (`saltspring-ontology`,
`social-knowledge-service`, `matchmaker`). Nothing here has been tested
against a user.

## The through-line

People reason about the world with a small vocabulary of kinds (a ship, a
port, a government, a storm) and a smaller vocabulary of parts (a thing has a
name, a place, a lifespan, an owner). The news is a continuous stream of
instances of those kinds. This tool watches the stream and tries to recover
the vocabulary from it, then shows the vocabulary alongside the instances,
and admits when the vocabulary needs to change.

The product principle from the house: deterministic core, generative edge.
The store of entities, claims and provenance is the auditable core; models
propose extractions, merges and schema changes, and every proposal carries
its evidence, its author (which model) and its confidence, so a later pass
can undo it.

## Personas

- **The reader.** Someone who wants to see what is going on in the world as
  things on a map, over time, rather than as a list of headlines. Opens the
  site, drags the time slider, clicks a thing, reads what the sources said
  about it. No login.
- **The ontologist.** Anselm, or anyone interested in how categories form.
  Wants to see the prototypes the machine proposed, how many entities sit
  under each, which components they share, and where the scheme is
  straining (the platypus cases). Reads the notes to follow the reasoning.
- **The operator.** The session running the pipeline. Needs to see source
  health, backlog per phase, which model ran what, and to re-run a phase
  after a schema change without re-fetching the world.

## User stories

1. As a reader I open the site and see a map of the last week's named
   places and the things that happened there, with a slider to move the
   window back in time.
2. As a reader I click a thing and see its label, what kind of thing it is,
   where it was and when, and the sentences from the sources that mention
   it, each with a link to the article.
3. As a reader I see the things it is related to (owns, governs, sailed
   through, opposes) as a small graph around it.
4. As an ontologist I open the prototypes page and see the kinds in use,
   their counts, their soft hierarchy, and the components each kind
   typically carries.
5. As an ontologist I open the components page and see each component's
   fields and how many entities carry it.
6. As an ontologist I read the notes page: short, dated, plain entries on
   what the machine decided and why, written for a reader who did not watch.
7. As an operator I see the sources table with last poll, next poll,
   failures and backoff, and the counts of content waiting at each phase.
8. As an operator I can mark a phase dirty for everything (after changing
   the component scheme) and watch the worker chew back through it.

## App areas

- `/` map with time slider; selection opens a detail panel; related
  entities drawn as a small graph.
- `/graph` the relationship graph for the current time window.
- `/prototypes` and `/components` the vocabulary views.
- `/sources` and `/pipeline` the operator views (public; nothing secret).
- `/notes` dated reader-facing notes; `/about` what this is.

## Milestones

1. **Survey and design** (this week): literature read, module map written,
   component and prototype starter sets chosen with their evidence.
2. **Inhale**: sources table seeded from a curated list, scheduler with
   backoff polling feeds, content stored raw with provenance and a content
   hash; a sources page showing health.
3. **Pre-analysis**: each content item tagged with entity mentions,
   relations, sentiment and time/place hints by a model, stored as a
   separate `analyses` record with the model name; a page that paints the
   tags over the text.
4. **Entities**: resolution of mentions into entities with arbitrary ids,
   aliases as evidence, merges recorded as events; a crude point plus
   radius for places; the map and time slider.
5. **Prototypes and components**: induction of kinds and parts from the
   accumulated entities, shown on their own pages; a dirty-flag re-run
   after a scheme change.
6. **Live**: exe.dev VM, ontology.monster, worker on canada1 or the VM.

## Not in scope now

Editing by users, accounts, exact boundaries for places, non-text media,
languages other than English, anything resembling a feed reader for humans.
