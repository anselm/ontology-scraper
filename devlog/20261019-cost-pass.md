# 2026-10-19: the cost pass

Point-in-time: what was measured on 2026-10-19 and what was changed. Rests on the OpenRouter key and credits endpoints,
the live `analyses` collection, the worker journal on the VM, and `bin/eval-models.mjs` runs saved in the session
scratchpad. Follows `20261014-first-build.md` (which measured 5.7k in + 3.6k out tokens per article on Opus 5.5).

## What was actually happening

- **Analysis cost.** 323 articles read by Opus 5.5 between 10-14 and 10-18: 1.86M input and 1.11M output tokens, about
  $30 at list price ($4/M in, $20/M out). Output was three quarters of it. The daily budget of 100 held (71, 96, 97
  articles on 10-15 to 10-17).
- **The schema was sent twice** (in the system text and as the response format), about 1.5k tokens per call for nothing.
- **Credits ran out on 10-18**, and from the first night OpenRouter had also been refusing with 402 "requires more credits,
  or fewer max_tokens" whenever the balance dipped below the 16,000-token reservation. The pipeline counted every 402 as
  a document failure, so after three the document was marked `failed` for good: **1,474 documents were dead** and the
  worker idled with nothing claimable, even after the key worked again. 3,703 refusals were logged on 10-18 alone.
- 151 of 1,805 extracted documents (8%) were the same story from a sibling feed (NPR twice, DW top and all).
- The OpenRouter key is shared with other projects, so the $66 "monthly usage" it reports is an upper bound for this one.

## What changed

- `lib/analyze/llm.mjs`: a 402, 429 or 5xx from OpenRouter pauses that tier for 15 minutes (1 minute for rate limits)
  and throws `unavailable`; `extract.mjs` then **releases the claim** (`pipe.release`, attempts untouched) instead of
  failing the document. The system prompt is sent with `cache_control: ephemeral` (Anthropic models through OpenRouter
  honour it: 1.8–2.8k of ~4k input tokens come back as cache reads). The schema goes once, as the response format.
  `max_tokens` default 8192 (the mean output is 2.5–4k). OpenRouter's per-call `cost` is stored in `usage.cost`.
  `ask()` takes a `model` override, and `ollama:<name>` routes to the local tier, for evaluation.
- `extract.mjs`: a document whose title an analysed document already carries is skipped as a duplicate. Text is cut
  at 9,000 characters (was 14,000; the mean is 5.2k). Spans under 100 characters, quotes under 160. A second attempt
  goes to `OPENROUTER_MODEL_ANALYZE_FALLBACK`. A re-run analysis sets resolve back to pending (before, a prompt bump
  would have produced analyses nothing resolved). **PROMPT_VERSION 2**: kind conventions written down (a polity is a
  place even when it acts; a journal, paper or database is a work; a university, company or agency is an agent; a
  disease or species is a thing) and an instruction to be exhaustive about named people and organisations.
- `bin/eval-models.mjs`: runs the production prompt with any model over documents that have a reference analysis and
  scores entity precision and recall (by name or alias key), kind and prototype agreement on matched entities,
  relation recall, tokens, dollars. Writes nothing. `bin/unfail.mjs <phase>` puts billing-failed documents back.
- `lib/service.mjs` `spend()` and a spend table on `/pipeline` (older rows priced from the list rates, marked *).
- `deploy/secrets.sh` copies the per-phase model variables and `ANALYZE_BUDGET` through to the VM.

## The measurement

Two reference sets. "Opus" is 30 recent articles with their Opus 5.5 analyses (prompt v1); "hand" is the 8 DW
articles analysed by hand on 10-14, the only human reference there is. Precision: share of the candidate's entities
that match a reference entity; recall: share of the reference's entities found. Kind and prototype agreement are over
matched entities. "Rel pair" is the share of reference relations where the candidate related the same two things by
any predicate. Cost is OpenRouter's billed figure per article, with the cache warm.

Readings:

- **Sonnet is Opus** for this job, at half the price: same recall against the human reference, same kind agreement,
  within two points on prototype.
- **Haiku is the most precise and the best at top kinds** (99–100% agreement with the human on kind, better than Opus's
  95%), and 25 times cheaper than Sonnet. Its weakness is **recall**: it lists about three quarters of what a person
  listed, missing minor named people and institutions, and it relates fewer things. One article in eight hit the
  output cap (a runaway), which is what the fallback is for.
- Haiku's "kind disagreements" with Opus were mostly Opus's choices: country as agent (Haiku: place), journal as
  agent (Haiku: work), disorder as event (Haiku: thing). The v2 conventions settle these the way Haiku had them, which
  is also what the resolver needs (see the Russia split in `20261014-polity-as-agent.md`).
- The non-Anthropic cheap models were worse and less reliable: GPT-5 mini ran past the output cap on 9 of 30, DeepSeek
  returned predicates outside the enum on 2 and hit the cap on 1, Gemini was fine on reliability but a third of its
  entities matched nothing in the reference.
- A local gpt-oss 120b run on canada1 against the hand set was still going when this was written; its result is
  appended below if it finished before the commit.

## Decision

Haiku 5.5 for `analyze` and `judge`, Sonnet 5.5 as the second-attempt fallback, Opus 5.5 kept for induction (the
rare adjudication of the vocabulary). `ANALYZE_BUDGET` 400 a day, which covers every new article (260–430 a day) and
drains the 1,800-document backlog in about four days. Expected spend: about **$0.60 a day** for analysis against
roughly $10 before, and the backlog about $3.50 once. The recall loss is partly repaired over time, since a thing missed
in one article is usually named in another, and precision matters more than recall to a vocabulary that is meant to
settle. If recall turns out to matter more, `OPENROUTER_MODEL_ANALYZE=anthropic/claude-sonnet-5.5` is the switch, at
about $15 a day for everything.

Deployed 17:40 UTC; the first Haiku analyses landed within the minute and `bin/unfail.mjs analyze` put 1,470
documents back to pending.

## Not done, and why

- **Batch pricing.** OpenRouter lists `:batch` variants at half price, and the Anthropic Batches API is also half
  price with caching on top; the pipeline is a one-document-at-a-time claim loop and would need a batch phase. Worth it
  only if volume grows ten-fold; at Haiku prices it is cents.
- **Local models on canada1 as a free tier** via `bin/chores.mjs`: possible, but the VM cannot run them and the
  measured 120b result is in the table (or not) above.
- The 331 documents analysed under prompt v1 will be re-read under v2 by Haiku (claim picks `done` under an older
  version), which double-counts mentions for those; acceptable, noted.
- Judge quality on Haiku was not measured separately; the judge sees short pairs and the resolver treats a refusal as
  "new thing", the safe direction.
